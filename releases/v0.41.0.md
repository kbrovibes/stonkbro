# v0.41.0 — Paper desk: personas, memory, and a backfill harness

## Summary
The ten paper-trading bots stop being rule sets and start being traders. Each gets a desk name, a role, a creed and a drawn face; each keeps a persistent memory of what it has lived through, written after every close. Underneath, three engine changes: a bot short of buying power now closes its weakest holding to fund a trade instead of logging a rejection, the two margin bots got hard equity floors, and a new in-memory backfill harness can replay past sessions against recorded bars with synthetic option chains. The Paper screens were rebuilt around the personas.

## Soul (persistent memory)
`paper_memory` stores what a bot remembers, separate from the daily `paper_notes`. `src/lib/paper/memory.ts` derives six kinds from each close:

| Kind | What it captures |
|---|---|
| `creed` | The founding belief. Never earned, never lost. |
| `conviction` | A belief the record keeps confirming. |
| `lesson` | Something the bot learned from how a trade went. |
| `scar` | A loss worth not repeating. |
| `streak` | A run of days in one direction. |
| `milestone` | An equity or trade-count threshold crossed. |

Rows are unique on `(profile_id, kind, headline)`: re-observing a memory bumps `hits` and `last_seen` rather than inserting a duplicate, so a repeated lesson gets heavier instead of the list getting longer. The runner writes memories after the close session.

**Memory is never read back into order generation.** Strategies decide from quotes, history and account state exactly as before. Memory is character, not signal.

## Buying-power management
`src/lib/paper/funding.ts` gives every profile one shared behaviour: an order rejected for buying power triggers a close of the weakest thing already on the book, then a retry. "Weakest" is the worst unrealised return across candidates. Two guards are absolute:

1. Nothing closes that would leave a short leg uncovered — shares backing a covered call and the long LEAPS under a PMCC are untouchable while the short call is open.
2. Multi-leg structures close as a whole; a single wing is never lifted off a condor.

Both the funding sale and the funded trade record reasons saying what happened. `src/lib/paper/engine.ts` now executes closing orders before opening ones, so a bot's own exits free capital within the same session. `scripts/validate-paper-funding.ts` asserts the behaviour across 26 checks.

## The broker will not strand a short call
`executeOrder` in `src/lib/paper/broker.ts` rejects any order that would leave a short position uncovered: selling the shares behind a covered call, closing the LEAPS under a PMCC while its short call is open, or lifting one leg of a multi-leg group while a short leg of that group is still there. Strategies close the short leg first, but that was a convention rather than a guarantee — a rejected close still let the cover be sold. The rejection reason reads `Would leave a short leg uncovered`.

It also refuses to *write* a short call the account cannot cover, with the reason `No shares or longer-dated long call to cover this short call`. That guard is separate because `marginMode: "covered"` declares a margin requirement of zero, so the buying-power test had nothing to reject and a naked call opened for free.

The funding layer's first guard and this one overlap on purpose. Funding chooses not to offer such a position as a candidate; the broker refuses it however the order arrives.

## Equity floors
- **Vector** (`growth-shadow`) previously added 25% of equity on margin on every 3% basket drop with no limit. It now stops adding within 5% of an **$85,000 equity floor**, never borrows more than **50% of equity**, and sells down to zero borrowing under the floor.
- **Breakwater** (`put-seller`) stops opening new puts under the same $85,000 floor.

## Backfill harness
No provider serves a historical option chain, so the harness models one. `src/lib/paper/synthetic.ts` prices chains with Black–Scholes off realised volatility from the bars; `src/lib/paper/backfill.ts` replays sessions entirely in memory against recorded daily bars and never touches Supabase. `src/lib/paper/invariants.ts` holds the safety checks shared by the validator and the report.

| Script | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `scripts/paper-fetch-bars.ts` | Cache the daily bars a window needs |
| `scripts/paper-backfill.ts` | Replay a window across all ten bots |
| `scripts/paper-report.ts` | Write the HTML + Markdown report |
| `scripts/validate-paper-invariants.ts` | Assert the engine's safety invariants |
| `scripts/validate-paper-funding.ts` | Assert the buying-power behaviour (26 checks) |

## Notes
- `20260909010000_paper_memory.sql` was applied to the stonkbro Supabase project. RLS: authenticated read, service_role manage — same shape as the other `paper_*` tables.
- Profile ids did not change, so no data migration was needed for the rename.
- The backfill harness is a local analysis tool. It runs from `scripts/`, writes nothing to the database, and is not wired to any cron or route.
- Synthetic chains are a model, not recorded quotes. Backfilled option results are indicative and are not comparable to the live sessions the crons record.


## Post-audit hardening

A static read of the strategy source, done after the first cut of this work, found three paths
that a simulated month could not reach. All three are fixed and all three now have regression
tests, because the scenarios need conditions a single directional month does not produce.

- **Longview could be left holding a naked short call, permanently.** When a LEAPS delta drops
  under 0.55, `pmcc.ts` emits a buy-to-close for the short call followed by a sale of the LEAPS.
  The close needs buying power and the sale does not, so a rejected close left the short call
  standing alone — and `leapsOf()` gates all short-call management on the LEAPS existing, so that
  symbol became invisible to the strategy forever. The trigger is cross-symbol: a short call on
  one name settling deep in the money drives cash negative, and a cheap close on a second name
  then rejects. That needs one name rallying and another falling in the same window, which is why
  neither a rising nor a falling month produced it.
- **Longview could grow a ratio spread by one contract per session.** `needsShort` was set from
  the intent to close rather than the result, so a rejected buy-to-close still triggered a fresh
  short sale against the same LEAPS.
- **Canopy could be left short a naked strangle**, by the same shape: short closes need buying
  power, wing sales do not.

The fix is two guards in `broker.ts` rather than three strategy rewrites. `executeOrder` refuses
any sale that would strand a short leg (by cover or by group), and refuses to write a short call
the account cannot cover. Verified by `scripts/validate-paper-funding.ts`, which reproduces each
scenario directly.

Two further findings, both about de-risking that does not stick:

- **Booster de-risked and re-levered in the same tick.** `momentum.ts` excluded the positions it
  was selling from its holdings count, which opened a slot, then bought a replacement at 32% of
  equity against buying power that still included the full margin line. Its $85K floor never
  actually held. A tick that de-risks now returns immediately.
- **Breakwater had no ceiling on total obligation.** Contract sizing floors at one contract, so an
  expensive name wrote more notional than intended. Total short-put obligation is now capped at
  2.5× equity.

### What the stress window still breaks

Run over 2025-01-15 to 2025-04-08, 59 sessions in which SPY fell 15.9%, every safety check holds
except one: Vector's equity falls 28% below its starting capital, past the 25% line the checks
draw. Its equity floor fires and cuts borrowing to zero, so it de-levers as designed and the
account survives, but a concentrated unhedged growth basket on margin is the desk's
highest-variance sleeve and it behaves like one. That is reported rather than tuned away.
